# Publication and release handoff

Use this reference when preparing a manuscript or public code/data release.
Apply the checks that fit the intended deliverable and known recipient
requirements. Record the outcome in the existing spec or assessment; no new phase,
checklist file, manuscript format, or archive layout is required.

## Identify what readers receive

Identify the precise versions of the report, code, inputs or access instructions,
selected outputs, environment specifications, and supporting evidence in the
handoff. Reuse the selected inventory and run records; link their preserved
identities rather than copying them into a parallel manifest. A code revision
alone does not identify uncommitted edits or external data.

Give retained material a durable preservation and access route suited to its
size and restrictions. A mutable branch or temporary cluster directory alone is
not an archived release. Record an actual release version, archive identifier,
or accession when available; label a planned deposit as pending. Keep restricted
material's access procedure and reproduction limits explicit rather than claiming
that every input is public. Preserve evidence needed to interpret selection,
including relevant exploratory findings, even when it is not part of the public
bundle.

Record known code/data licenses and third-party access or redistribution terms.
Identify unresolved terms before proposing distribution; do not invent a license
grant or infer permission from the ability to download a file. For a prepared
handoff awaiting deposit, state who or what must resolve any remaining release
dependencies using the project's existing records.

## Check the assembled report and credit

Build or render the intended deliverable when it has a build step, and inspect
the actual artifact. Check figures and tables, legible labels and complete legends,
units and uncertainty, and resolved citations and cross-references. Reuse
Validation's result-to-evidence checks; record unverified rendering or inaccessible
artifacts explicitly.

Use `dunnlab-biblio` for verified literature, dataset, and software citations,
including the versions used where relevant. For manuscripts, verify author and
contribution information, acknowledgments, data/code availability text, and
applicable AI-use or other reporting statements against project-provided facts
and actual recorded work. Leave unresolved authorship or contribution decisions
to the researchers rather than inventing them.

## Exercise the reader's route

Check the canonical instructions from a fresh checkout or unpacked handoff in an
isolated workspace. Verify that readers can locate the stated versions, obtain
accessible inputs, reconstruct the required environment, and launch the documented
bounded check or dry run where supported. Use an appropriate small execution when
it provides missing evidence. Record which access or reconstruction steps were
actually tested and any unavailable steps.

Reuse valid clean execution evidence for the selected computation; a handoff check
does not require repeating a full expensive analysis. Conversely, a bounded check
does not establish a new full reproduction. Route genuine gaps through the
existing lifecycle rules and keep unaffected evidence.

State whether the assessment covers scientific readiness, a prepared handoff, or
a completed release/submission. A pending deposit can coexist with a prepared
handoff; it cannot be reported as completed publication. Perform external actions
within existing authorization and do not add another permission gate solely
because the lifecycle assessment passed.

The preservation, identification, access, and credit checks draw on
[FAIR4RS](https://www.rd-alliance.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/FAIR4RS20principles20v1.020final.pdf)
and the [FORCE11 software citation principles](https://force11.org/info/software-citation-principles-published-2016/).
They guide the handoff without prescribing a universal repository or publication
process.
